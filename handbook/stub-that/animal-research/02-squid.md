# AN-002 — Squid

**Current research state:** comparative cephalopod stub; the exact species and tests remain to be selected.

**Biology.** Many squid use muscles controlled by neural signals to expand or retract chromatophore pigment organs, and reflective tissues such as iridophores can also influence skin appearance. Their optical and physiological behavior varies by species.

**Do not conflate:** pigment expansion, active reflection changes and an observer's lighting-angle effect are distinct mechanisms. Do not claim all squid express the same timing or controls.

**Candidate measurements:** stimulus, response time, skin region, pigmentation/reflectance, angle and illumination, recovery.

**Chromatophore learning:** one qualified state change can coordinate pigment and optical responses through separate effectors, while preserving underlying identity.

**Source:** [Chromatophore biological comparison](../../../BIOLOGICAL-TRANSFORMATION-AND-COMPUTATION-20261122.md). Species-specific literature and reproducible measurements still needed.
