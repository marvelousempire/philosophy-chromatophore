# Cross-study — Optical shift and perception

**Main distinction:** an observer can see a different color even when the observed organism's underlying physical structure has not changed.

1. **Object/material:** pigment, scale structure, skin cells, texture and condition.
2. **Illumination:** source spectrum, brightness, angle and polarization.
3. **Optical interaction:** selective absorption, reflection, scattering, interference and diffraction.
4. **Viewing geometry:** direction and distance; camera or eye position.
5. **Reception and perception:** light reaching the eye/camera and, for humans, visual processing in the brain.
6. **Observed appearance:** the resulting hue, brightness, gloss, iridescence or pattern under those conditions.

### Comparison
- **Octopus:** the animal can actively alter its skin pattern.
- **Chameleon (studied species):** the animal can actively alter reflective-cell arrangements.
- **Butterfly (some structural colors):** a stationary wing's apparent hue or brightness may change because the lighting or viewing angle changes, not because the wing changes biologically.
- **Butterfly metamorphosis:** a genuine developmental change in tissue and body form, distinct from the optical effect.

**Warning:** viewing angle does not always produce the same optical shift; geometry and species matter. A digital approximation is not a precise measurement of light or consciousness. To validate real optical behavior, use actual measurements and defined viewing conditions.
